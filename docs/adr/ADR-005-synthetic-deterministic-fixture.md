# ADR 005: A seeded synthetic fixture, and what it cannot stand in for

Status: accepted

## Context

To measure whether a leakage detector fires on a leak and stays quiet on a clean series,
you need a ground-truth switch for the leak: a way to generate the same series with and
without the leak present. No real demand dataset gives you that switch, because on real
data you never know for certain whether a leak is present. The fixture also has to be
reproducible: a reviewer must get the same numbers from one command, offline, with no
keys.

## Decision

The series is generated by a seeded numpy process: a drifting level with periodic regime
shifts, a weekly seasonal shape, and autoregressive noise. The non-stationarity is
deliberate and tunable, because it is what gives a future-aware feature something to leak
and what creates the null band the gap is calibrated against. Every figure in the
repository is measured over a corpus of clean series indexed by seed, with disjoint seed
blocks for calibration, clean evaluation, and leaked evaluation, so no series both sets a
threshold and is scored against it.

## Alternatives considered

- **A public retail dataset.** Rejected as the default: it has no ground-truth leak switch,
  it is not offline or license-clean to vendor, and its own hidden leaks would confound the
  measurement. It would make a good second fixture for a transfer study (see the Pivot).
- **A fully stationary synthetic series.** Rejected because it would make the null band
  trivially tight and hide the hardest part of the problem, which is separating a leak from
  a drift.

## Consequences

Every operating-characteristic number, detection rate and false-alarm rate, is a statement
about this process, not about any particular real series. What transfers is the mechanism
and the discipline: the gap between a shuffled and an ordered backtest, the calibration of
that gap against clean reference series, and the refusal to certify clean without the power
to do so. What does not transfer is a specific detection rate, because that depends on how
non-stationary the real series is and on how large the real leak is. The README's boundary
section states this, and the one claim that genuinely needs real data, that these rates
hold on production demand series, is explicitly out of reach here.
